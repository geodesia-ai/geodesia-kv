\begin{abstract}
\noindent
KV-cache compression is usually evaluated along one of two axes: resident
capacity for quantization and token eviction, or bytes fetched for sparse
attention. Treating these quantities as interchangeable can make a method
appear smaller than it is. A second confounder is chunked teacher forcing:
although logits remain causal, a quantizer such as KIVI can treat an entire
evaluation chunk as its current exact residual. We present a corrected,
token-incremental study of \method, a family of training-free cache policies
that combines monotone blockwise precision allocation, a small exact
rate--distortion residual, and query-sparse reading using exact page summaries.
All policies share a causal attention path, grouped-query attention (GQA)
statistics are aggregated before allocating the shared KV head, and resident
bits are reported separately from bits read per query.

At 16k context on \wikitext, the approximately five-bit operating point
improves on a local KIVI-4 port for Qwen2.5-3B
($6.320$ versus $6.322$ perplexity at $4.96$ versus $5.03$ \bpv) and Qwen3-8B
($10.540$ versus $10.581$ at $4.99$ versus $5.03$ \bpv). The margins are
small. On a frozen six-book \pg{} transfer, the same 8B point loses
($16.258$ versus $16.147$), so it is not a multi-corpus dominance result.
Conversely, compressed-Quest is slightly worse than Quest on six \wikitext{}
windows, but on six preregistered \pg{} books it improves perplexity
($16.931$ versus $17.022$), resident rate ($9.83$ versus $16.25$ \bpv), and
read rate ($1.95$ versus $2.32$ \bpv). An exploratory non-incremental transfer
to a Qwen3-30B-A3B GPTQ-Int8 checkpoint fits an RTX A6000 and favors a
$2.061$-\bpv{} low-rate point, but is not primary evidence.

We additionally validate a packed, head-batched attention kernel in isolation.
It reduces the represented cache by approximately $6.9\times$ and is faster
than a local dense \texttt{bmm} reference, but it is not yet integrated into
Transformers' \texttt{past\_key\_values}; therefore we make no end-to-end
throughput or peak-VRAM claim. The resulting picture is mixed but actionable:
the sparse branch transfers across corpora, the five-bit branch does not, and
the remaining scientific and systems gaps are explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive attention stores one key and one value for every layer and
past token. Its memory therefore grows linearly with context length even when
the model weights have already been compressed. This creates two distinct
resource problems. First, the \emph{resident cache} must fit in device or host
memory. Second, decode must \emph{read} enough of that cache for every query.
Quantization primarily attacks the former; query-sparse methods such as
Quest~\cite{tang2024quest} primarily attack the latter. Reporting a sparse
method's selected bytes as if they were its resident capacity conflates the
two.

Evaluation can introduce a second conflation. In a realistic decode, the model
appends one token and issues one query at a time. In chunked teacher forcing,
an implementation may append tens or hundreds of target tokens in one call.
The logits can still be causally masked, yet the cache policy may see the
whole chunk as a current, unquantized region. For KIVI, for example, its
effective resident rate in our implementation grows from $5.03$ \bpv{} for a
64-token chunk to $5.69$ for a 1024-token chunk. Comparisons made with
different query lengths are therefore not rate matched.

This work began as an attempt to allocate a graded precision ladder to the
entire context. The investigation uncovered and corrected future-query leakage,
duplicated allocation of GQA-shared KV heads, a missing prefill observer,
incorrect handling of partial Quest pages, contaminated teacher-forced
retrieval, and resident/read accounting errors. These corrections materially
changed the empirical conclusions. Rather than preserve an obsolete claim of
universal dominance, we report the method that survives, the settings that do
not transfer, and the missing system integration.

\method{} now denotes three related operating branches:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Graded allocation}: every block remains represented on a
      monotone ladder of exact, scalar-quantized, or centroid states.
\item \textbf{Graded+RD}: a small exact residual is selected from the prompt by
      attention mass times squared value-reconstruction error.
\item \textbf{Compressed-Quest}: exact key boxes rank pages at query time, but
      the resident K/V pages themselves use the graded representation.
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item A causal, Q=1 evaluation protocol with explicit separation of resident
      rate and read rate, model/text revisions, per-window results, and
      regression tests for query leakage and GQA sharing.
\item A monotone rate--distortion allocator over a heterogeneous precision
      ladder, including the exact scale overhead and a prefix constraint on
      demotion paths.
\item A target-free exact residual whose saliency combines prompt attention
      mass with value distortion, and a query-sparse variant that stores exact
      page boxes while compressing the underlying K/V.
\item Primary incremental results on 3B and 8B checkpoints, plus a frozen
      cross-corpus \pg{} test over the exhausted preregistered window list,
      with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals. The intervals separate an
      effect that survives from one that was an artifact of a six-window
      subset.
\item A numerically checked packed multi-head kernel microbenchmark and a
      measured account of resident context capacity, including the finding
      that grouped query attention makes device memory a non-binding
      constraint on maximum context for these checkpoints.
\item A record of negative quantum-inspired and conventional variants,
      including spectral K--V states, cumulant correction, Born-style gating,
      Hadamard rotation, VQ, static protection, and layer profiles.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Eviction and retained-token methods.}
StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streaming} combines attention sinks with a recent
window. H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} retains heavy hitters. SnapKV~\cite{li2024snapkv}
uses the final prompt queries to score tokens before generation.
PyramidKV~\cite{cai2024pyramidkv}, PyramidInfer~\cite{yang2024pyramidinfer},
CAKE~\cite{qin2025cake}, and Ada-KV~\cite{feng2024adakv} distribute eviction
budgets across layers or heads. These methods can attain very low resident
rates, but an evicted token cannot answer a later query.

\paragraph{Query-sparse attention.}
Quest~\cite{tang2024quest} stores elementwise key minima and maxima for each
page, bounds the largest possible query--key score, and reads top-ranked
pages. InfLLM~\cite{xiao2024infllm}, MInference~\cite{jiang2024minference},
RetrievalAttention~\cite{liu2024retrievalattention}, and
InfiniteHiP~\cite{lee2025infinitehip} use representative tokens, structured
sparsity, approximate retrieval, or hierarchical pruning/offload. Their
resident backing store and query traffic must be measured separately.

\paragraph{KV quantization and merging.}
KIVI~\cite{liu2024kivi} uses per-channel key quantization along time,
per-token value quantization along channels, and an exact residual window.
KVQuant~\cite{hooper2024kvquant} and GEAR~\cite{kang2024gear} add non-uniform
datatypes, sparse corrections, or low-rank structure. CaM~\cite{zhang2024cam},
D2O~\cite{wan2024d2o}, and MiniCache~\cite{liu2024minicache} merge tokens or
exploit cross-layer similarity. \method{} combines scalar quantization and a
centroid state under one rate constraint, then optionally adds an irregular
exact residual.

\paragraph{Architectural and systems alternatives.}
DeepSeek-V2's latent attention~\cite{deepseek2024v2}, Native Sparse
Attention~\cite{yuan2025nsa}, Compressive Transformers~\cite{rae2019compressive},
Infini-attention~\cite{munkhdalai2024infini}, and state-space
models~\cite{dao2024mamba2} change training or architecture. Our setting is
training-free and targets frozen Qwen checkpoints~\cite{qwen25,qwen3}.
FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention} supplies the online-softmax
recurrence used by our prototype kernel. GPTQ~\cite{frantar2023gptq} is used
only for the weights of the exploratory 30B checkpoint; KV-cache rates remain
independently measured.

\paragraph{Long-context evaluation.}
RULER~\cite{hsieh2024ruler} and LongBench~\cite{bai2023longbench} cover
retrieval and downstream tasks that perplexity alone cannot establish. We use
\wikitext{} and \pg{} for controlled language-model loss, and treat the
absence of RULER/LongBench as a limitation rather than as evidence of memory
fidelity.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Causal policy state and GQA sharing}

Let a decoder call contain queries $q_0,\ldots,q_{Q-1}$ and a cache prefix of
length $T$. The representation serving $q_0$ may depend only on the prefix and
$q_0$; using $q_1,\ldots$ to demote blocks leaks future teacher-forced
information. \method{} therefore allows only the current query to trigger a
demotion. Later queries may update statistics only for subsequent calls.

During prefill, a separate observer collects causal attention mass from the
final prompt queries. This initializes both block masses and token saliency.
For GQA, multiple query heads share one KV head. Their observed masses are
aggregated first and the physical KV representation is allocated once.
Allocating separately per query head both invents non-existent storage and can
demote the same cache repeatedly.

\subsection{Precision ladder and exact rate}

The cache is partitioned into blocks of $P=64$ tokens. A block occupies one
level
\[
\mathcal{L}=\{16,8,4,2,1\},
\]
where 16 is exact \texttt{bf16}/\texttt{fp16}, 8/4/2 are packed scalar
quantizers, and 1 denotes a single centroid K/V pair repeated logically across
the block. Keys are grouped along time and values along channels, following
the asymmetry established by KIVI. Each level cost $\beta_b(L)$ includes
payload, offsets, scales, exact sink/window blocks, and any separate residual.

A newly closed block can only be demoted. It is never promoted from its
reconstruction because the original values would no longer exist in a real
compressed store. This monotonicity makes the simulated policy implementable
without silently retaining a dense copy.

\subsection{Equipotential allocation}

Let $m_b$ be the exponential-moving-average attention mass of block $b$, and
let $e_b(L)$ be a range/radius proxy for reconstruction error at level $L$.
The allocator solves
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{L_b\}}\sum_b m_b^\gamma e_b(L_b)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_b\beta_b(L_b)\leq B,
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma=1$ in the final configurations. The Lagrangian relaxation
decouples blocks through
\[
m_b^\gamma e_b(L)+\lambda\beta_b(L).
\]
Each possible one-step demotion has a marginal damage per saved bit. We sort
these ratios and find the threshold that reaches the budget. A prefix rule is
mandatory: reaching level 2 from 16 requires accepting the 16$\rightarrow$8,
8$\rightarrow$4, and 4$\rightarrow$2 steps for that block. Without this
constraint an early implementation reported budgets it had not actually
paid.

\subsection{Rate--distortion exact residual}

Pure block allocation can miss a small number of high-impact tokens. For each
prompt token $j$, the prefill observer records attention mass $a_j$ and its
distance from the value centroid of its block. The final residual score is
\begin{equation}
s_j=a_j\left\lVert v_j-\bar v_{b(j)}\right\rVert_2^2.
\label{eq:rdscore}
\end{equation}
The highest-scoring prompt tokens are duplicated exactly. This selection is
target-free at inference: it uses only prompt activations and no continuation
labels. Its full 16-bit K and V cost is added to the graded base. The
five-bit points use:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item Qwen2.5-3B: base $B=4.875$, decay $0.25$, exact fraction
      $0.78125\%$;
\item Qwen3-8B: base $B=4.5$, decay $0.5$, exact fraction $3.125\%$.
\end{itemize}

A lower-rate variant, RD-V2, uses a nearly centroid-only base and an
approximately $10.2\%$ exact residual selected by the same value-distortion
score. It is included only in non-incremental exploratory tables.

\subsection{Compressed-Quest}

Quest's sparse reads do not imply a compressed resident cache: exact K/V pages
remain resident. Our \texttt{box\_sparse} branch stores, for every closed
block, exact \texttt{fp16} key minima and maxima computed before quantization.
For a query $q$, the box upper bound ranks all closed pages. The current
partial page is always readable; no extra sink/window page is forced outside
the top-$k$ set. Cold pages receive a hard mask.

The resident representation is the graded K/V cache plus all exact summaries:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{resident}}
=R_{\mathrm{graded}}+\sum_b 2D\cdot16\;\text{bits}.
\end{equation}
The per-query read cost is all summaries plus the graded K/V of selected and
current pages:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{read}}(q)
=R_{\mathrm{boxes}}+
\sum_{b\in\mathcal{S}(q)\cup\{\mathrm{current}\}}R_b.
\end{equation}
The final 8B setting uses base $B=10$, decay $0.5$, and a $12.5\%$ page
fraction. Exact boxes contribute approximately $0.25$ \bpv{} but avoid ranking
on quantized summaries.

\subsection{Attention error certificate}

For reconstructed keys and values, write $\hat a$ for compressed-cache
attention weights and $\hat v$ for reconstructed values. The output error
decomposes as
\[
o-\hat o=\sum_j\hat a_j(v_j-\hat v_j)
          +\sum_j(a_j-\hat a_j)v_j.
\]
With a score-error bound $\varepsilon_b$ and value-error bound
$\delta_b^v$ for block $b$,
\begin{equation}
\|o-\hat o\|
\leq \sum_b\hat a_b\delta_b^v
+\left(\frac{M^+}{M^-}-\frac{M^-}{M^+}\right)
\max_j\|v_j\|,
\label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
where $M^\pm=\sum_b\hat a_b e^{\pm\varepsilon_b}$. The second term is capped by
the universal $\ell_1$ bound of 2. Validation runs with the dense reference
enabled produced no observed violations. Hard sparse masking yields a valid
but generally loose universal bound, so the certificate is currently more
informative for graded reconstruction than for \texttt{box\_sparse}.

\subsection{Packed attention prototype}

The packed prototype dequantizes inside an online-softmax attention loop and
batches all KV heads of a layer. A separate reduction combines split-context
partials. Numerical tests use distinct queries for multiple heads and agree
with a PyTorch reconstruction reference within $10^{-4}$ relative tolerance.
This is a kernel prototype, not a Transformers cache implementation: quality
experiments still retain dense K/V to simulate policies, and incremental
append/demotion is not wired into \texttt{past\_key\_values}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Models, corpora, and hardware}

Primary experiments use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct revision
\texttt{aa8e7253} and Qwen3-8B revision \texttt{b968826d}, both at a 16,384
token context on one NVIDIA RTX A6000. Activations are \texttt{bfloat16}.
An exploratory scaling run uses community checkpoint
\texttt{JunHowie/Qwen3-30B-A3B-GPTQ-Int8} revision
\texttt{059b0db2} in \texttt{float16}; its weight quantization is separate from
the KV policy. A Qwen3.5-0.8B checkpoint is retained only as a diagnostic
pilot because it predates the final incremental protocol.

\wikitext{} validation and test files are pinned by SHA256. For the final
test file the hash is
\begin{center}\vspace{-4pt}
\texttt{bbf94c53a05abe9ee670d3b634360809}\\[-2pt]
\texttt{5822c85e26de37c70b24fc571964574a}.
\vspace{-4pt}\end{center}
The 3B test uses six disjoint offsets from 0 to 81,920. The frozen 8B test uses
six later offsets from 196,608 to 278,528.

\pg{} is fetched from \texttt{emozilla/pg19} at dataset revision
\texttt{c021754c8e01c5b1cc83a1f549c1f97fbbb756b8}. Per-book boundaries and
hashes are stored in manifests. The test text contains 12 books and has SHA256
\begin{center}\vspace{-4pt}
\texttt{42a0b97ff79ec96f53e18ec14bd770464}\\[-2pt]
\texttt{5bd10b7c50125935dbae721e9e8b445}.
\vspace{-4pt}\end{center}
Test offsets are the preregistered per-book centers: each window is the 16,384
token span centered in one book. Of the twelve books, eleven admit a full
window; the fifth contains only 6,679 tokens and is excluded. We report all
eleven, so the \pg{} test list is exhausted and no window selection remains
available. The offsets are 20,668, 90,369, 169,602, 246,823, 320,206, 402,659,
472,081, 529,495, 584,324, 659,151, and 736,259. An earlier version of this
work reported the first six of these; Section~\ref{sec:paired} shows why that
subset was insufficient.

\subsection{Incremental perplexity}

For each window we:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item prefill the exact 16k prefix while collecting causal observations;
\item issue one query for each teacher-forced target token;
\item append that token to the same policy state;
\item accumulate the negative log-likelihood of the next token.
\end{enumerate}

Every primary result uses $Q=1$ and 64 target tokens per window. Equal-size
windows are aggregated by averaging NLL, equivalently taking the geometric
mean of perplexities. A deterministic fake causal model regression test
checks target alignment between chunked and incremental implementations.
Real \texttt{bf16} full-cache PPL can differ by about $0.45\%$ between $Q=64$
and $Q=1$ due to numerical kernel ordering, so methods are compared only
within the same mode.

\subsection{Paired confidence intervals}
\label{sec:paired}

Aggregate perplexities alone cannot separate a real effect from sample noise,
and the margins at stake here are fractions of one percent. Because every
policy evaluated on a given window is teacher forced on exactly the same
targets, the per-token negative log-likelihoods of two policies are paired.
Their mean difference is exactly the log ratio of the two perplexities,
\begin{equation}
\log\frac{\mathrm{PPL}_a}{\mathrm{PPL}_b}
  = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\ell^{a}_i - \ell^{b}_i\bigr),
\label{eq:paired}
\end{equation}
so a confidence interval on the mean paired difference maps directly onto one
for the perplexity ratio.

Tokens inside one window share a prefix, a cache state, and an authorial
style, so resampling individual tokens would be anticonservative. We therefore
use a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole windows with replacement
($20{,}000$ draws, percentile interval). The small number of clusters is
reflected honestly in the resulting width rather than hidden. Runs are
byte-reproducible: re-executing the eleven-window \pg{} test reproduces the
previously published six-window aggregates of $16.147$ and $16.258$ exactly.

\subsection{Metrics and baselines}

We report:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item resident bits per scalar K or V value, including metadata;
\item bits read per value per query;
\item teacher-forced perplexity;
\item attention KL to the dense policy in separate diagnostic runs;
\item represented cache bytes and attention-only latency for the prototype.
\end{itemize}

Full-KV, StreamingLLM, SnapKV, Quest, and KIVI are local semantic ports
implemented on a shared attention path. They are not executions of the
original version-pinned kernels. Quest stores exact resident K/V plus page
summaries; KIVI includes scale overhead and its exact current residual;
SnapKV statistics use the final prompt queries. This common implementation is
valuable for causal comparison but does not replace an official-code systems
benchmark.

The six-book subset suggested that the five-bit point fails to transfer,
losing $0.69\%$ of PPL. Exhausting the preregistered list removes that
conclusion (Table~\ref{tab:pgfive}). Across all eleven books the margin
shrinks to $0.10\%$, the win count moves from $2/6$ to $5/11$, and the paired
95\% interval on the perplexity ratio contains one. On $693$ paired targets
the interval half-width is roughly $0.9\%$, an order of magnitude larger than
the effect being measured.

The defensible statement is therefore neither dominance nor failure: at
$4.989$ against $5.031$ \bpv{} the two policies have no measurable perplexity
difference, so \method{} attains the same quality at lower resident capacity.
We report this because the earlier negative claim and any symmetric positive
claim are equally unsupported by a sample of this size. The same caution
applies to Table~\ref{tab:primary}, whose $0.04\%$ and $0.39\%$ margins have
not yet been given intervals and are of the same order as the effect that
dissolved here.

The exact RD residual remains useful in the rate sense: on three of the books,
a mistakenly executed pure graded control used $4.486$ \bpv{} and scored
$24.307$, whereas the corrected $3.125\%$ residual used $4.985$ \bpv{} and
improved to $24.222$. KIVI-4 scored $24.121$ on the same books. The residual
score in Equation~\ref{eq:rdscore} adds information, but a globally fixed
base/fraction has not been shown to beat KIVI-4 across corpora.

\subsection{Resident context capacity}
\label{sec:capacity}

The preceding tables measure rate. This section measures what that rate buys
in device memory, using real allocations rather than the simulated quality
path. We load the model with 4-bit NF4 weights, measure the resident weight
bytes and the peak activation of a genuine $Q=1$ decode step, and then locate
the largest allocatable context by bisection. For Full-KV the search executes
a real decode step at every probe, so its result is a demonstration rather
than an estimate; for compressed formats we allocate buffers of the computed
size, which establishes residency but not execution.

One number validates the accounting used throughout this paper. Building the
packed format from real model K/V, with the level assignment chosen by the
real allocator, measures $1.959$ \bpv{} where the accounting reports $1.990$.
The rate accounting is therefore mildly conservative rather than optimistic.

Table~\ref{tab:capacity} supports a conclusion that is largely negative, and
we state it rather than the flattering reading. Both checkpoints use grouped
query attention with eight and two KV heads respectively, so their caches cost
$144.0$ and $36.0$ KiB per token. On the A6000 an uncompressed Full-KV cache
reaches $294{,}911$ tokens for the 8B with real decode executed, which is
$7.2\times$ the model's native window. For these models on this class of
device, maximum context is bounded by the trained position window, not by
cache memory. Compression changes the ranking without changing feasibility.

There is one practical exception. At the $131{,}072$ token ceiling reachable
with RoPE scaling, the 8B needs $23.67$\,GiB for weights plus an uncompressed
cache, which does not fit a 16\,GiB device; the measured Full-KV limit there is
$70{,}655$ tokens. The same window costs $7.99$\,GiB with the two-bit branch
and $11.33$\,GiB with KIVI-4. Compression thus unlocks the model's full
supported window on 16\,GiB accelerators. For the 3B no such point exists: its
full window costs $6.42$\,GiB uncompressed, and even a 16\,GiB device holds
$413.7$k Full-KV tokens, more than three times the reachable ceiling.

Comparing the two model families in Table~\ref{tab:capacity} isolates what
capacity depends on. Within a model the ratios between methods are fixed by
rate alone, so \method{} 2-bit exceeds KIVI-4 by $2.44\times$ at both 8B and
3B; what changes across models is only the absolute scale, through the
$4\times$ difference in bytes per token induced by two versus eight KV heads.
Capacity headroom is therefore a property of the attention architecture first
and of the cache policy second. A method that halves the rate cannot
compensate for a checkpoint that stores four times more per token, which is
why the 3B reaches larger contexts than the 8B under every policy including
the uncompressed one.

\section{Negative Results and Lessons}
\label{sec:negative}

\paragraph{Chunked evaluation as a false optimization signal.}
Increasing the evaluation chunk made KIVI's current exact residual larger and
changed both rate and PPL. Final comparisons therefore moved to Q=1.

\paragraph{Static prompt protection.}
Exact prompt blocks, Snap-like token tails, and centroid gates improved some
calibration windows but failed disjoint holdouts. A $2.061$-\bpv{} exact-tail
hybrid nearly matched SnapKV during development and then lost on holdout.

\paragraph{Quantum-inspired spectral states.}
We tested a block state containing dominant key covariance modes and K--V
cross-covariance response. Batched power iteration made the prototype faster,
but ranks one through four worsened PPL relative to the scalar ladder.

\paragraph{Cumulant correction.}
A signed second-order correction for quantization inflation and centroid
Jensen deflation improved one validation window and worsened another. A
single global correction does not transfer.

\paragraph{Born-style projection.}
Selecting the smallest block subspace containing a target fraction of
centroid softmax mass improved several validation windows, then failed the
frozen test. Composing it with RD-V2 was slower and worse than RD-V2 alone.

\paragraph{Hadamard rotation and key/value asymmetry.}
Hadamard incoherence processing~\cite{tseng2024quip,ashkboos2024quarot}
spread key outliers into channels already protected by per-channel scales and
degraded quality. Allocating more bits to keys at the expense of values also
lost; value error was not negligible.

\paragraph{Vector quantization and fine groups.}
Multiple centroids help only below approximately $1.5$ \bpv{}. Similar keys do
not imply similar values, so reusing key clusters leaves large value error.
At practical rates, more scalar bits with coarser groups beat two-bit payloads
with expensive fine-grained scales.

\paragraph{Uniform layer profiles.}
Several six-group layer profiles were tested at matched mean capacity.
Uniform allocation was better or indistinguishable. This does not rule out
head/layer policies learned from a larger multi-corpus validation set; it
rules out the tested hand-designed profiles.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Small evaluation sample.}
The \pg{} test now uses all eleven preregistered windows and reports paired
intervals, but $693$ targets still give a 95\% half-width near $0.9\%$ of the
perplexity ratio, while the effects at stake are $0.1$--$0.7\%$. The
\wikitext{} tables remain at six windows and have no intervals at all; their
margins should be read as unresolved rather than established. Because the
\pg{} list is exhausted, added power must come from further corpora, not from
further windows of this test.

\textbf{Local baseline ports.}
Shared semantics expose causal bugs and allow matched accounting, but original
baseline releases and optimized kernels must still be executed where
possible.

\textbf{Incomplete task coverage.}
No final RULER, LongBench, or multi-key retrieval suite is reported.
Perplexity and attention KL are insufficient to establish long-term retrieval
fidelity.

\textbf{No real compressed model cache.}
Quality runs simulate compressed states while retaining dense K/V. The packed
kernel is isolated and supports neither real incremental append nor integration
with \texttt{past\_key\_values}. We therefore do not claim end-to-end speed or
serving throughput. The capacity results of Section~\ref{sec:capacity} measure
real allocation and, for Full-KV, real decode, but the concurrency counts are
cache-only arithmetic rather than a served workload.

\textbf{Mixed cross-corpus result.}
Compressed-Quest transfers on \pg{} with an interval that excludes one, but
misses \wikitext{} PPL. The five-bit point is statistically undecided against
KIVI-4 on \pg{}. No current point beats every baseline on every measured axis
from 3B upward, and on the perplexity axis several comparisons are simply
below the resolution of the available sample.

\textbf{Exploratory 30B protocol.}
The 30B checkpoint fits and produces favorable chunked results, but Q=1
incremental replication, official baselines, and system measurements are
still required.

\section{Conclusion}

\method{} is best understood as a measured family of rate--distortion and
query-sparse cache policies, not as a completed universal replacement for
every KV technique. The causal Q=1 protocol establishes small \wikitext{}
improvements over a local KIVI-4 port at 3B and 8B, which currently lack
confidence intervals. On the exhausted \pg{} window list the five-bit point is
statistically undecided against KIVI-4, delivering equal perplexity at lower
resident capacity; the earlier claim that it fails to transfer was an artifact
of a six-window subset. In the complementary sparse regime, compressed-Quest
reduces resident storage and query traffic and improves \pg{} perplexity with
an interval that excludes one, though it does not improve \wikitext{}
perplexity.

The capacity measurements reframe what these rates are worth. Because grouped
query attention has already made the cache small, device memory does not bound
maximum context for either checkpoint, except when a 16\,GiB accelerator must
hold the 8B at its RoPE-extended window. Compression instead multiplies
concurrency, by $8.8\times$ over Full-KV at the native window. That is the
axis on which the measured advantage is a factor rather than a fraction of a
percent, and it is where we would direct further work.

The next algorithmic step is not further tuning on these test windows. It is a
layer/head allocation policy calibrated only on multi-corpus validation and
then frozen for a larger paired test, sized in advance for the effect it needs
to resolve. The next systems step is a true packed
\texttt{past\_key\_values} implementation with append, partial blocks, GQA
sharing, monotone demotion, and sparse page reads. Only after comparison with
FlashAttention/PagedAttention and official baseline kernels should
end-to-end speed and throughput claims be made.
